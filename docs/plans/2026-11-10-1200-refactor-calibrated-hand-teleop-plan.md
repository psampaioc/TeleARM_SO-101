# Calibrated RGB hand teleoperation for SO-101

## Goal Capsule

**Objective:** Make a measured displacement of the operator's palm produce a corresponding displacement of the SO-101 gripper center along agreed right/up/forward control axes, with visible estimates, bounded error and one supervised activation gesture. The camera need not see the robot or remain in a fixed position relative to it between sessions. Prepare the hand orientation frame for a later single-pose IK target.

**Means:** Keep one Python process and the installed OpenCV, MediaPipe and LeRobot stack. Separate the main loop, hand geometry, camera/coordinate math, state and target selection, FK/IK, motor I/O, and presentation by responsibility. Calibrate the camera and hand scale from independently measured positions; compare simple depth estimators before selecting one. Reuse the existing SO-101 URDF and LeRobot kinematics. Coordinate physical work through the companion [TarefasHumanas](../TarefasHumanas.md) plan.

**Authority and stop conditions:** The current `AGENTS.md`, `project/SAFETY.md` and `project/bring-up-checklist.md` govern hardware work. The saved LeRobot calibration remains authoritative for servo limits and IDs. If the hand estimate fails held-out validation, retain preview and the current bounded control mode; do not claim metric 3D or send unvalidated targets. Live physical motion requires the operator beside the arm with the workspace clear and the power cut-off accessible.

## Product Contract

### Problem

The current pipeline anchors wrist pixel position and an apparent 0–9 hand span, then multiplies normalized image changes by empirical gains. Horizontal direction has been observed; vertical motion drifts while the hand is stationary, and depth and gripper motion are not physically validated. `hand_controller.py` mixes geometry, camera lifecycle and overlay. The present URDF and LeRobot FK/IK already describe the robot's nominal 3D geometry; the missing pieces are a measured hand displacement, an explicit control-axis convention and a measured offset from the URDF gripper frame to the actual grasp center. No spatial registration between webcam and robot is needed for relative control.

### Requirements

- R1. Deliver one hand-control pipeline in the existing single-process runtime, container and dependencies. Preserve LeRobot's serial/calibration ownership, PALM activation, FIST/HOLD, joint bounds and the independent gripper command. The old empirical mapping and `--metric-profile` switch are migration scaffolding only; remove the old mapping and switch after the measured relative controller passes its gates, so the final live command has one behavior.
- R2. Keep `main()` as the CLI entry point and `run()` as the one resource-owning loop: capture, MediaPipe inference, feature extraction, controller update, rendering/logging and cleanup. Move hand arithmetic, calibrated coordinates and presentation out of that loop, with explicit image pixels, hand-relative 3D coordinates, camera metres and robot-base metres.
- R3. Show a small red reference point on the palm, calculated from stable palm landmarks 0, 5, 9, 13 and 17 or their polygon centroid. Do not use the mean of all 21 points for translation, because finger curl would move the reference while the palm stays still. Keep numbered landmarks in saved diagnostic frames, not on the normal live image.
- R4. Calculate palm and fist classification, continuous triangle 4–9–12 jaw angle, a palm normal and a longitudinal palm axis. Reject low-quality or occluded geometry instead of inferring a trustworthy target from it.
- R5. Measure camera intrinsics and lens distortion at the actual capture resolution and fixed focus. Record independently measured palm depth and lateral/vertical displacements, plus image samples at several distances, image locations and hand orientations. Never treat MediaPipe world landmarks or the operator's gesture labels as absolute camera-space ground truth.
- R6. Compare stable projected palm span 5–17 with palm-landmark polygon area for geometric robustness. Use calibrated depth as an aid to X/Y back-projection, but use **relative apparent scale from the accepted PALM** as the Z control input. Calibrate a comfortable scale-ratio endpoint in preview; 1.5× nearer and its reciprocal 1/1.5× farther are starting candidates, not accepted thresholds. A real silhouette area would require segmentation outside this KISS scope.
- R7. Convert the chosen palm image center to relative camera X/Y displacement with calibrated intrinsics/scale, and map it to robot-base control axes with an explicit signed axis convention, independent of the physical camera-to-robot pose. At PALM activation, anchor the hand center, apparent span and measured robot tool center. Human travel of approximately ±20 cm on X/Y and a **virtual** ±20 cm Z command derived from the scale ratio are the input ranges, not robot-space caps: scale the normalized input continuously toward the reachable tool-center boundary given the existing calibrated motor and URDF joint limits, including simultaneous-axis movements.
- R8. Obtain the tool center from LeRobot FK of the five arm joints and a physically measured fixed transform from `gripper_frame_link` to the midpoint between the jaws. Validate the transform at several static robot poses. Motor 6 controls jaw opening and does not by itself locate the tool center.
- R9. Send one pose target to the existing IK for the five arm joints and a separate gripper action through the existing LeRobot adapter. Preserve the calibration/URDF intersection, step limits and feedback states; do not add ROS, TCP, MuJoCo or another servo driver.
- R10. Keep the camera image minimal: one compact `GESTURE | CONTROL` line, small red palm reference and one small loop-latency number. Distinguish `PALM | ACTIVE`, `FIST | HOLD`, `HAND | READY`, `NO_HAND | HOLD` and `HAND | INVALID` as applicable; show `INVALID` while rejecting bad geometry and never imply that the arm follows it. Put numbered landmarks in saved diagnostic frames. Put the detailed hand XYZ estimate, robot readback FK, anchor, requested/capped target, IK residual, jaw angle, bounds, quality reason and per-stage timings in the shared terminal as structured live lines. Label estimates, commands and readback distinctly; never cover the hand with a diagnostic panel.
- R11. After XYZ is accepted physically, map palm normal plus longitudinal axis into a constrained tool orientation in the **same** target pose and **same** IK call; report unreachable orientations rather than iterating translation and tilt alternately.

### Acceptance examples

- AE1. With the palm stationary, opening and closing fingers changes jaw angle but does not shift the red palm center or create a Cartesian target beyond measured jitter.
- AE2. Moving the calibrated palm 10 cm right or up should request roughly half the available continuous travel in that direction from the PALM anchor; 20 cm should request the reachable boundary. For Z, a measured apparent-scale change corresponding to half or all of the configured virtual range should request the same fractions; do not label that virtual input as a measured 10/20 cm of physical hand depth. Combined directions share the actual coupled workspace, so the final distance may differ by direction and anchor pose. Report measured X/Y, scale ratio, normalized input, computed reachable boundary and physical tool motion separately. The webcam may be relocated between sessions and PALM anchors anew.
- AE3. Rotating the palm in place changes the normal and projected area; the depth estimator either remains within its validated error or marks the sample invalid and holds the last safe command.
- AE4. At PALM 3/3 the system anchors hand X/Y, apparent palm span and robot tool-center FK without jumping; FIST immediately requests HOLD; loss of hand never creates a new target.
- AE5. If the chosen camera scale, explicit control-axis convention or FK offset is unavailable, live metric mode refuses to arm and explains the missing input. Preview remains available. No camera-to-robot extrinsic calibration is required.
- AE6. The live video remains readable and shows only the selected gesture/state, small red palm center and a small delay number; numbered points appear only in saved diagnostic frames. The terminal contains the measurements and rejection reasons needed to diagnose movement without asking the operator to transcribe a crowded HUD.

## Planning Contract

### Architecture and ownership

```text
teleop/hand_controller.py: main → run; owns one OpenCV/MediaPipe/robot lifecycle loop
                          camera frame → MediaPipe 21 points
                               ↓
teleop/hand_geometry.py: MediaPipe result → immutable hand sample; pure palm center,
                        span/area, gesture, jaw angle, palm frame and quality
                               ↓
teleop/camera_mapping.py: calibrated K/distortion + fitted hand scale → metric camera X/Y;
                          explicit signed camera/control-axis mapping for relative movement
                               ↓
teleop/controller.py: PALM/FIST state, anchors, bounded target, gripper, HOLD
                               ↓
teleop/kinematics.py: LeRobot FK/IK + gripper-frame-to-tool-center transform
                               ↓
teleop/lerobot_adapter.py: existing SO101Follower, calibrated .pos read/write

teleop/overlay.py: consumes sample/controller/timing snapshots; one compact video status,
                   small red palm point/latency, structured terminal diagnostics; no control logic
```

`hand_geometry.py` owns feature extraction and the hand-sample type, `camera_mapping.py` owns camera calibration and 3D coordinate math, and `overlay.py` owns presentation because the present HUD/log code occupies a substantial part of `hand_controller.py`. None owns serial state or IK. A one-time `scripts/calibrate_hand_camera.py` captures/reads measured samples, fits and evaluates the camera profile under ignored `data/`; it is not a second live service. The runtime reads a small versioned calibration profile; no model training or new neural inference is required. Existing private imports from `hand_controller.py` remain as compatibility aliases during the offline extraction; migrate test callers when test changes are explicitly authorized.

**Configuration and code contract:** keep algorithm defaults together at the top of their owning module, with units in each name or adjacent schema field. Put *measured* camera intrinsics, distortion, hand scale, valid working volume, axis convention and tool-center offset in one versioned local profile under ignored `data/`; keep a tracked example with `null` for unknown measurements. Do not invent values, use `null` as zero, or activate metric live control with an incomplete/profile-mismatched file. Validate dimensions, finiteness, units, profile version, camera mode and operator identity at load. Keep the existing LeRobot motor calibration separate and unchanged. Prefer pure functions for geometry and transforms, one owner for capture/serial, explicit frame names (`I`, `C`, `B`, `G`, `T`), and single-purpose modules; do not add dependencies, ROS/TCP or a second driver. Preserve existing public CLI and state/error behavior while extracting code. Every refactor must update its callers and nearby fixtures in the same unit; no silent fallback from calibrated to empirical control. Physical measurements and acceptance evidence remain placeholders until the named H gate.

### Coordinate and measurement contract

Use four named frames: image pixels `I` (unmirrored source), camera `C` (X right, Y down, Z away from lens), robot base `B`, and gripper/tool `G/T`. Rendering may mirror the picture, but mirroring must never silently change measurement coordinates: make the selfie-view sign explicit in the control-axis mapping. MediaPipe image `x,y` are normalized; multiply by capture dimensions and undistort before metric back-projection. Its world points are a hand-centered shape estimate, not the translation of the hand relative to the webcam.

For a chosen, validated palm span `s` in undistorted normalized-camera coordinates, estimate `Z_C ≈ L_eff/s` only as needed to back-project X/Y; compare this with the palm-landmark area candidate. `L_eff` and any area scale are fitted effective dimensions tied to this camera, person and palm geometry. They do not define the Z control command. At PALM, store `s0`; then set `r=s/s0` and use a smooth monotone scale command, initially `z_fraction=clip(log(r)/log(r_near), -1, 1)`. If preview supports `r_near=1.5`, then 1.5× apparent span is full toward-camera input and 1/1.5× is full away-camera input. Choose the actual endpoint from observed visibility, noise and orientation rather than assuming 50% will work. In the front-facing approximation, projected area carries an orientation factor `|n·view|`; rotating a hand without translating it can look like depth change. Reject or hold those samples rather than steering Z from them. Calculate metric X/Y on undistorted pixels with the auxiliary depth estimate; do not claim the virtual Z command measures physical hand travel.

On PALM activation store `p_C0`, apparent span `s0` and the translation of `T_B_T,0 = FK_B_G(q_read) · T_G_T`. Normalize measured X/Y displacement by 0.20 m and apparent-scale change into a virtual Z displacement `0.20 m × z_fraction`; pass those three values through the **same** 3D control mapping. Clip only the input fractions to `[-1, 1]`; `M_control` maps their direction into the robot base. For each requested direction, find the furthest continuously reachable tool-center pose from the anchor at the chosen tool orientation under the existing LeRobot-calibrated motor and URDF joint limits. Scale the distance to that boundary by the largest absolute input fraction, so half of a 20 cm X/Y excursion or half of the configured Z scale-ratio range requests half of the reachable travel along that direction. A diagonal request must use the coupled 3D reachable boundary, not three independent axis maxima; reject a direction if a reliable continuous boundary cannot be found. Keep small per-step rate limits, but remove the legacy fixed ±0.10 m Cartesian command cap from this mode. `M_control` is a documented signed axis permutation: operator/screen right, screen up and hand toward the webcam map to agreed robot right, up and forward axes. Its signs account for the mirrored preview, but it is **not** a measured camera-to-robot rotation. Reconstruct a tool pose with the anchored/selected orientation, then convert it for the existing IK using `T_B_G,target = T_B_T,target · inverse(T_G_T)`; solve **once per control update**. The offset is expressed in gripper coordinates and rotates with the wrist, so it must not simply be added in base axes. The camera can be anywhere relative to the robot because no absolute hand/robot alignment is used. If the operator moves the camera during an active gesture, its apparent hand position changes; the operator must request HOLD before moving it and re-anchor with PALM afterward, because this webcam-only pipeline cannot distinguish camera motion from hand motion. The robot base must stay mechanically stable during motion, but the setup can be relocated between sessions. The current URDF already has link geometry and the fixed `gripper_frame_link`; only the actual grasp-center offset and model accuracy need measurement. If the physical jaw center shifts materially with opening, document or model that offset before promising exact center tracking.

The palm normal comes from a cross product of non-collinear palm points; its sign depends on handedness and winding. Combine it with the wrist-to-middle-base axis to build an orthonormal hand frame and a pointing direction. A normal alone leaves free rotation about that normal. The SO-101 has five arm joints, so arbitrary six-degree-of-freedom tool poses may be unreachable; prioritize position and apply only achievable orientation components when U7 is enabled.

### Ground truth protocol and decisions

1. Set the webcam to the actual 640×480 capture mode, with stable focus and zoom during the calibration capture. Its physical position relative to the robot is irrelevant. Use `docs/calibration-board-9x7-18mm.svg`: A4, 10×8 squares, 9×7 **internal corners**, nominal 18 mm squares. Print at 100%, measure the printed side and mount flat. Aim for 20 accepted views in varied positions/tilts (15 fit, 5 held out); minimum 15 accepted (12 fit, 3 held out), above OpenCV's guidance of ≥10 good fit patterns. Calibrate `K` and distortion. Provisional quality gate: fit reprojection RMS ≤1 pixel and held-out p95 ≤2 pixels; repeat poor captures rather than accept a high-error profile. Recalibrate if webcam intrinsics change (device, focus, zoom or resolution), not because the laptop or robot was moved. Human steps H01–H03 provide the camera settings and physical board/poses.
2. Independently measure the lens-to-palm reference distance with a ruler/rig at several distances (proposed 30, 40, 50 and 60 cm, with 35/55 cm held out if visible), plus center/left/right and front/tilted palm views. At two usable depths, put a ruler-marked horizontal/vertical grid behind the palm, hold its plane approximately parallel to the camera sensor, and capture measured 5/10 cm lateral and vertical palm-center shifts while keeping depth approximately constant. Record grid tilt, the palm-to-grid gap and placement uncertainty; reserve complete shift sequences for evaluation. Capture three stationary holds of about 30 consecutive frames per fitted distance and stationary held-out sequences. Split evaluation by whole distance/orientation/shift **session**, never random frames from the same hold. A ruler-measured visible palm width is a rough cross-check, not the exact under-skin 5–17 landmark length. FIST, side and jaw poses are useful negative/quality cases, not standalone metric depth labels. Human step H04 supplies these measurements.
3. Reserve complete poses and at least one distance/angle and lateral/vertical shift sequence for held-out evaluation. Fit an effective hand scale only to support X/Y projection; report its depth diagnostic error, relative lateral/vertical displacement error, jitter and bias by pose. Separately validate that the selected apparent-span ratio gives a monotone, low-jitter Z command across the intended near/far gesture and remains stable when the palm rotates without translation. If either check fails, restrict valid poses/range or pause replacement control; do not force an area formula or call the virtual Z physical centimetres.
4. Define and document a fixed, signed permutation `M_control` from upright selfie-view right/up/toward to the robot's intended right/up/forward control axes. Identify the robot axes from its URDF once; do not fit camera-to-base extrinsics. With the robot still, have the operator move the hand right/up/toward in the webcam view and confirm that each measured `Δp_C` has the expected sign and magnitude. In H07, verify each mapped robot motion physically and correct a sign only from that evidence. A new laptop/robot placement requires a new PALM anchor, not another camera-to-robot calibration. If the webcam is rolled, the image/control convention must be stated or the image returned upright.
5. Identify a visibly fixed fiducial that maps to the CAD/URDF (preferably the `wrist_roll` axis center) before measuring the physical midpoint between the gripper jaws. Measure its three local offsets, several static arm poses and two jaw openings with scale-bearing orthogonal photos/ruler if needed. Derive `T_G_T` from the URDF geometry and those relative measurements. Separately compare FK predictions with **base-referenced** physical tool-center observations in multiple poses; relative gripper photos alone do not validate absolute FK. Existing LeRobot calibration gives encoder limits, not proof of absolute geometric accuracy. Human step H06 supplies physical observations.

### Dependencies and risks

- Existing `/dev/video0`, MediaPipe model, LeRobot calibration JSON, URDF and container are reused. No new container image or dependency is assumed.
- User participation is needed to place the hand at independently measured distances and to supervise physical arm movement. Work through offline/refactor and preview stages before this gate.
- Intrinsics or hand scale can become invalid if the webcam device, focus, zoom, resolution or operator changes; relocating an unchanged webcam does not alter intrinsics. A saved profile needs its capture resolution and calibration identity checked on load. A moving camera during active control creates false hand displacement; HOLD and re-anchor before moving it.
- Occlusion and rotation can make 2D scale ambiguous. The quality gate must hold commands rather than turn ambiguity into movement.
- `RobotKinematics` currently reports `ok` after solver progress even if its Cartesian residual remains large; verification must calculate the tool-center residual explicitly before accepting a command.
- Preserve the current physical safety behavior while moving code. Never infer successful movement from a planned target or a serial write alone; compare readback FK and operator observation.

### U7. Add pointing only after XYZ passes

**Covers:** R4, R11. **Human gate:** H07 must pass first; H08 supplies supervised orientation observations. **Files:** `teleop/hand_geometry.py`, `teleop/camera_mapping.py`, `teleop/controller.py`, `teleop/kinematics.py`, nearest tests and checklist. Anchor a full palm frame from normal and longitudinal axis. Derive relative hand rotation `ΔR_C = R_hand,current · R_hand,anchor⁻¹`, then map it as `ΔR_B = M_control · ΔR_C · M_control⁻¹` and apply it to the anchored tool orientation in the **same** tool pose solved in U5. Conjugation keeps `ΔR_B` a proper rotation even if the selfie/control sign matrix has determinant −1; multiplying the hand frame directly by such a matrix would create an invalid reflection. This controls pointing relative to the activation pose; it does not claim absolute world alignment of hand and gripper. No post-IK joint adjustment or XYZ/tilt feedback loop. Keep the jaw command independent. **Pass:** isolated orientation changes preserve tool-center position within accepted tolerance; unreachable directions produce visible rejection/hold. **Fail:** position drift, joint-limit clipping that changes XYZ or ambiguous normal sign.
**Code practice:** make orientation an explicitly disabled profile feature until H07/H08; keep position and orientation in one target pose and one IK call, with a visible rejection if unreachable. Do not add a compensating post-solve joint loop.
